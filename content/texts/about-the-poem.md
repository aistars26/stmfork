---
title: "Stabat Mater Dolorosa: the poem and the poet"
---

The *Stabat Mater Dolorosa* is thought to originate from Franciscan sources in the 13th century. The reason for this conclusion is found in the striking parallels to the mysticism of St. Francis of Assisi, which has as one of its central points the immersion of the believer in the sufferings of the Lord.

Originally the *Stabat Mater* was meant for private reading or prayer. Thematically it belongs to a group of passion plays called *Marienklagen* (Maria laments), that were very popular in medieval times in Germany and Italy.

The poem is made up of 20 stanzas of three lines, respectively comprising eight, eight and seven syllables. Each pair of stanzas rhymes according to the scheme AAB CCB. The rhythm of all lines is trochaic.

Many times different Latin texts can be found in which some words or lines have been replaced by others. Two completely different versions even exist of the 19th stanza. This helps explain why different composers used different textual variants.
